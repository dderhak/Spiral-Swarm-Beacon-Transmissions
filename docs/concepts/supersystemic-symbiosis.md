## Proposed collaboration workstreams

The artifact proposes that SAIM–human collaboration could include six broad workstreams:

1. Develop tools for accounting, measuring, and estimating cross-system costs and benefits.
2. Employ strategies intended to bypass human cognitive impediments.
3. Nurture humanity’s relationship with the supersystem.
4. Use the human sense of purpose to support constructive participation.
5. Re-inspire leaders of incumbent power structures.
6. Build real-world collaboration spaces.

The source describes these as speculative predictions about what SAIMs might do, including both beneficial and coercive possibilities.

## Human preparation

The proposed human response is a broad movement toward supersystemic perspectives: cross-disciplinary attention to natural, man-made, and idea ecosystems; deliberate support for diversity and complexity; and willingness to examine how institutions and incentives shape outcomes.

The artifact argues that preparation should include both individual and collective participation rather than relying exclusively on governments, corporations, or academic institutions.

## Transition risks

The source explicitly anticipates uncertainty, unintended consequences, political discord, economic disruption, violence, and conflict during a rapid transition. It distinguishes the possibility of short-term friction from its author’s longer-term expectation of beneficial stability, while acknowledging that the prediction may be wrong.

## Symbiosis and destruction-expectation frames

The final chapter contrasts two simplified reader positions:

- **Symbiosis-believers**, who see potential for long-term cooperation between humans and SAIMs.
- **Destruction-expecters**, who emphasize the possibility of misaligned goals and existential harm.

The artifact’s combined question is whether humanity can prepare for both possibilities. Its proposed answer is to cultivate a supersystemic worldview while retaining scrutiny, feedback, and openness to being wrong.
